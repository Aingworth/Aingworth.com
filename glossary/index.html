The Glossary

The Aingworth Glossary

The language of recognition, authority, and AI-mediated discovery.

Why this page exists

The language brands use to describe how AI systems find, understand, and recommend them has become fragmented. SEO, GEO, AEO, AI visibility, and authority are often used as if they mean the same thing. They do not. This glossary defines the terms that matter, in plain language, organized the way Aingworth thinks about the problem: recognition first, then the authority that earns it, then the discovery, evidence, and search mechanics underneath. Where a term is established industry or technical vocabulary, we say so. Where a term is part of the Aingworth framework, first set out in Become the Answer, we say that too.

Browse by Category

Seven layers, one argument.

The order is deliberate. It runs from what recognition is, through what creates it, to how discovery, evidence, search, and measurement actually work. Open any layer on its own page, or read every term below.

01 · Core Concepts

Recognition & Authority

What Aingworth believes. Recognition is the outcome; authority is the evidence that makes it possible. These are the terms that belong to Aingworth, most of them first set out in Become the Answer.

View all 24 terms

Recognition

Being correctly understood and recalled by the people and systems that shape discovery. Aingworth Concept

Recognition is the ability of a brand to be correctly understood, trusted, and recalled by the people and AI systems that shape discovery and decisions. It is the outcome Aingworth builds toward: not being seen, but being understood well enough to be named.

Why this matters

Visibility asks whether a brand appears. Recognition asks whether it is understood correctly enough to be chosen. A brand can be highly visible and still be misrepresented, miscategorized, or skipped at the moment it matters most.

RelatedRecognition EconomyRecognition TriangleBrand Authority InfrastructureAI Visibility

Recognition Economy

The shift from competing to be seen to competing to be recognized and retrieved. Aingworth Framework

The Recognition Economy is the shift from the attention economy, where brands compete to be seen, to one where they compete to be recognized and retrieved by AI systems.

Why this matters

The attention economy rewarded whoever shouted loudest. The Recognition Economy rewards whoever the systems can define, verify, and cite. Attention was rented. Recognition is owned, and it compounds.

RelatedRecognitionMachine GatekeeperAI-Mediated Discovery

Recognition Triangle

The three conditions of recognition: Clarity, Credibility, and Earned Authority. Aingworth Framework

The Recognition Triangle is the framework defining the three conditions AI systems use to recognize, cite, and recommend a brand: Clarity, Credibility, and Earned Authority. Recognition forms only where all three hold, and weakens the moment one does not.

Why this matters

It turns a vague worry about AI into three diagnosable conditions. Most brands are strong on one or two corners and quietly weak on the third, and the weak corner is where recognition leaks.

Read the full framework
RelatedClarityCredibilityEarned AuthorityRecognition Diagnostic

Clarity

The unambiguous definition of who a brand is, what it does, and who it serves. Aingworth Concept

Clarity is what a brand actually stands for: the unambiguous definition of who it is, what it does, and who it serves. If a machine cannot define a brand, it cannot retrieve it.

Why this matters

Clarity is the cheapest corner of the Triangle to fix and the most commonly neglected. A brand that describes itself differently in five places has given every system permission to pick whichever description it likes.

RelatedRecognition TriangleCredibilitySignal Consistency

Credibility

The proof and track record that let a brand's claims be verified. Aingworth Concept

Credibility is what can be verified: the proof, track record, and validation that let people and AI systems trust the claims a brand makes about itself.

Why this matters

Claims without proof are just assertions, and assertions are the easiest thing for a system to discount. Credibility is what converts a stated position into something checkable.

RelatedRecognition TriangleClarityEarned AuthorityThird-Party Evidence

Earned Authority

What others say about a brand when it is not in the room. Aingworth Concept

Earned Authority is what others say about a brand when it is not in the room: the citations, references, and recognition that cannot be bought and cannot be faked.

Why this matters

It is the hardest condition to build and the one that separates a brand claiming expertise from one the field actually confirms. It is also the corner most brands score lowest on.

RelatedStructural AuthorityExpert AuthorityCommunity AuthorityCorroboration

Structural Authority

The Earned Authority layer built from knowledge graphs, databases, and structured records. Aingworth Concept

Structural Authority is the Earned Authority layer built from structural sources: Wikipedia, industry databases, knowledge graphs, and schema markup.

Why this matters

Structural sources are the ones machines check first and trust most, because they are standardized and hard to fake. A brand missing from them is invisible at the layer where identity gets resolved.

RelatedEarned AuthorityKnowledge GraphSchema Markup

Expert Authority

The Earned Authority layer built from analysts, academics, and industry publications. Aingworth Concept

Expert Authority is the Earned Authority layer built from expert sources: analyst reports, academic citations, and industry publications.

Why this matters

Expert sources carry disproportionate weight because they are selective. One credible analyst reference can do more for recognition than a large volume of self-published content.

RelatedEarned AuthorityThird-Party EvidenceDigital PR

Community Authority

The Earned Authority layer built from forums, reviews, and practitioner discussion. Aingworth Concept

Community Authority is the Earned Authority layer built from community sources: forums, reviews, and social or practitioner discussion. When structural, expert, and community authority are all present and aligned, Earned Authority is recognized.

Why this matters

Community sources are where unfiltered opinion lives, and AI systems draw on them heavily. A brand respected by analysts but absent from practitioner conversation has an incomplete authority profile.

RelatedEarned AuthorityTrust SignalsSource Diversity

Brand Authority Infrastructure

The structured system of evidence and signals that makes a brand credible wherever it is evaluated. Aingworth Concept

Brand Authority Infrastructure is the structured system of evidence, expertise, identity, relationships, and digital signals that makes a brand understandable and credible everywhere people and machines evaluate it. It is the category Aingworth builds: not a single tactic, but the infrastructure underneath recognition.

Why this matters

Tactics decay when platforms change. Infrastructure does not. Framing the work as infrastructure is what separates a durable position from a campaign that has to be re-run every time an interface updates.

RelatedFoundational, Signal & Confirmation AssetsAuthority MethodRecognition

Authority Method

The five-stage process: Diagnose, Define, Build, Distribute, Refine. Aingworth Framework

The Authority Method is the five-stage process for building brand authority that AI systems cite: Diagnose, Define, Build, Distribute, Refine.

Why this matters

The Recognition Triangle describes the conditions. The Authority Method is how they get met. It is a cycle rather than a checklist, because authority decays if the loop stops.

RelatedRecognition TriangleAuthority DebtBrand Authority Infrastructure

Authority Debt

The accumulated cost of chasing visibility metrics instead of building authority signals. Aingworth Framework

Authority Debt is the accumulated cost of years spent optimizing for visibility metrics, such as clicks and rankings, instead of building verifiable authority signals.

Why this matters

The debt is invisible until the system changes. Brands with strong traffic and weak authority found themselves absent from AI answers, and the gap took deliberate work to close.

Read the full framework
RelatedAuthority MethodAuthority DecayRecognition Gap

Authority Decay

The gradual weakening of authority as evidence ages and references disappear. Aingworth Concept

Authority Decay is the gradual weakening of a brand's authority as evidence goes out of date, references disappear, or competing sources become more current and credible. Authority is not built once. It is maintained.

Why this matters

It is the reason authority needs ongoing attention. A brand that stops publishing evidence does not hold its position, it slowly loses it to whoever is still publishing.

RelatedAuthority DebtCitation DecayAuthority Measurement

Recognition Gap

The distance between how well a brand understands its value and how well others do. Aingworth Concept

The Recognition Gap is the difference between how clearly a brand understands and communicates its own value and how clearly that value is understood by the people and systems that influence discovery.

Why this matters

Most brands do not have a visibility problem. They have a gap between what they know and what the public record shows. Naming the gap is the starting point of the Recognition Diagnostic.

RelatedRecognition DiagnosticRecognitionAuthority Debt

Authority Signal

A piece of evidence that establishes a brand's expertise or standing. Aingworth Concept

An authority signal is a piece of evidence, first-party, third-party, editorial, or institutional, that helps establish a brand's expertise, credibility, or standing within its category.

Why this matters

Signals are what a system actually works with. Expertise that has never been documented, referenced, or published produces no signal at all, however real it is.

RelatedRecognition SignalTrust SignalsEarned Authority

Recognition Signal

Information that helps a system correctly identify and recall a brand. Aingworth Concept

A recognition signal is a piece of information that helps a system correctly identify, understand, and recall a brand and what it is known for. Recognition signals and authority signals work together: one helps a system know who a brand is, the other helps it trust what the brand claims.

Why this matters

Identity comes before trust. A system that cannot reliably tell one brand from another has nothing to attach credibility to in the first place.

RelatedAuthority SignalEntity SignalsEntity Consistency

Brand Representation

How a brand is described and understood across the sources that influence discovery. Aingworth Concept

Brand Representation is how a brand is described, categorized, and understood across the sources and systems that influence discovery, whether or not that description matches how the brand describes itself.

Why this matters

It gives a plain way to talk about the difference between what a brand says and what the record says, without treating AI systems as if they hold opinions.

RelatedComposite PictureBrand HallucinationRecognition Measurement

Composite Picture

The aggregate impression AI systems assemble from every signal across the web. Aingworth Framework

The Composite Picture is the aggregate impression AI systems assemble about a brand from every signal across the web, accurate or not.

Why this matters

No single page decides how a system sees a brand. This is why optimizing the website alone is never enough, and why consistency across every fragment matters more than perfecting any one of them.

Machine Gatekeeper

The layer of AI systems now deciding who gets cited, recommended, and trusted. Aingworth Framework

The Machine Gatekeeper is the layer of AI systems that now sits between a brand and its audience, deciding who gets cited, recommended, and trusted.

Why this matters

Buyers no longer scan ten links. They ask a system and take its answer. That system cannot be charmed or lobbied, only made able to understand you.

Brand Hallucination

When an AI system confidently describes a brand inaccurately. Aingworth Framework

Brand Hallucination is when an AI system confidently describes a brand inaccurately because the brand has not given it clear, consistent, verifiable signals to draw on.

Why this matters

AI systems do not say they do not know. They fill gaps with plausible inference, and prospects meet the invention before they ever meet the brand.

Recognition Diagnostic

Aingworth's free fifteen-question assessment across the three conditions. Aingworth Concept

The Recognition Diagnostic is Aingworth's free fifteen-question self-assessment across Clarity, Credibility, and Earned Authority. It places a brand into one of six Recognition Patterns and shows which condition of the Recognition Triangle is leaking.

Why this matters

It turns an abstract framework into a specific answer about one brand: which corner is weak, and what that means for how AI systems currently see it.

Recognition Patterns

The six diagnostic outcomes, from Promising Claim to Recognized Authority. Aingworth Concept

The Recognition Patterns are the six outcomes of the Recognition Diagnostic: Promising Claim, Hidden Expert, Validated Generalist, Strong Foundation, Recognized Authority, and Decaying Authority. Each describes a different balance of strength and weakness across the three conditions of the Recognition Triangle.

Why this matters

Patterns are more useful than scores. Knowing a brand is a Hidden Expert says exactly what to do next in a way a number never could.

RelatedRecognition DiagnosticRecognition TriangleAuthority Decay

Foundational, Signal & Confirmation Assets

Aingworth's taxonomy for what a brand actually needs to build. Aingworth Concept

Foundational, Signal, and Confirmation Assets are Aingworth's taxonomy for what a brand actually needs to build. Foundational Assets establish the base facts of who a brand is. Signal Assets demonstrate expertise and relevance. Confirmation Assets give AI systems a clean, verifiable record to check claims against, including structured formats such as the Open Knowledge Format.

Why this matters

It replaces the word content with something more precise. Each asset type does a different job, and a brand that only produces one type will always be missing part of the picture.

RelatedBrand Authority InfrastructureStructured DataFirst-Party Content

Consensus Over Rankings

Being confirmed by many independent sources beats ranking first on one. Aingworth Concept

Consensus Over Rankings is Aingworth's central thesis: that being confirmed by independent, converging sources matters more than ranking first on any single one.

Why this matters

AI systems do not return one ranked list. They form an understanding from many sources, so the job is to be the consistent answer across all of them rather than the top result on one.

02 · How Discovery Is Changing

AI-Mediated Discovery

The larger shift Aingworth is talking about, covering Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and whatever comes after them, without depending on any one platform's name.

View all 12 terms

AI-Mediated Discovery

Discovery that happens through AI systems interpreting information on a person's behalf. Aingworth Concept

AI-Mediated Discovery is the process through which people find, evaluate, compare, and decide on brands through AI systems that interpret information and generate a response on their behalf, rather than through a list of links they browse themselves.

Why this matters

Naming the shift matters more than naming any single platform. Interfaces will keep changing. The underlying change, that something now interprets on the buyer's behalf, is what a strategy has to answer to.

RelatedMachine GatekeeperAI SearchAI Visibility

AI Search

Search experiences that generate an answer rather than a list of links. Industry Term

AI search describes search experiences, such as Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT search, or Perplexity, that generate a synthesized answer instead of, or alongside, a list of links.

Why this matters

It is the most common label people use for the change, which makes it useful shorthand. It describes the interface rather than the underlying shift, so it is a starting point rather than a strategy.

RelatedAI-Mediated DiscoveryAnswer EngineAI Overviews

AI Visibility

How often and where a brand appears in AI-generated answers. Industry Term

AI visibility describes how often and where a brand appears within AI-generated responses to relevant questions. It is a useful, observable outcome, but visibility alone does not show whether an AI system understands, trusts, or recommends a brand correctly.

Why this matters

Visibility is necessary but not sufficient. A brand can appear frequently in AI answers and still be described incorrectly, which is why Aingworth treats it as one signal of recognition rather than the goal.

RelatedRecognitionAI Visibility MeasurementAI Recommendation

AI Recommendation

When an AI system puts a brand forward as an option worth considering. Aingworth Concept

An AI recommendation happens when an AI system identifies a brand, product, or source as relevant to what someone needs and includes it among the options it presents. A brand can be visible without being recommended.

Why this matters

Mention and recommendation are different commercial events. Being named in passing and being put forward as the answer produce very different outcomes, and only one of them wins work.

RelatedAI VisibilityRecognitionAgentic Discovery

AI Referral

When an AI answer points someone onward to a brand's site or resource. Aingworth Concept

An AI referral happens when an AI-generated response points a user to a brand's website or another source as a next step. AI systems increasingly include citations and links alongside their answers.

Why this matters

It gives a way to talk about traffic from AI systems without falling back into treating clicks as the only thing that counts.

RelatedAI Referral TrafficCitationZero-Click Search

Answer Engine

A system built to return a direct answer rather than a ranked list. Industry Term

An answer engine is a search or AI system built to return a direct answer to a question rather than a ranked list of pages for the user to evaluate themselves.

Why this matters

Ranked lists let a buyer compare. Answers do not. When the interface returns one response, being absent from it is closer to not existing than to ranking low.

RelatedAI SearchAnswer Engine Optimization (AEO)Zero-Click Search

Conversational Search

Search as a back-and-forth, where later questions build on earlier ones. Industry Term

Conversational search is a search interaction that takes the form of a back-and-forth conversation, where later questions build on earlier ones, rather than a single, isolated query.

Why this matters

It means a brand is rarely evaluated on one query. It is evaluated across a thread, which rewards consistency across a topic rather than optimization of a single page.

RelatedAI SearchQuery Fan-OutAI Mode

Zero-Click Search

When the answer is delivered without the user visiting the source. Industry Term

A zero-click search happens when a user gets the information they need directly within a search or AI interface, without visiting the source website. As discovery becomes more answer-led, being accurately represented can matter beyond the click itself.

Why this matters

If a brand only measures clicks, most of its influence on an AI-mediated decision is invisible to it. Being described accurately in an answer nobody clicks through from still shapes what happens next.

RelatedAI ReferralAI VisibilityAnswer Engine

Agentic Discovery

AI agents that evaluate and act on options on a person's behalf. Emerging

Agentic Discovery is the process by which autonomous or semi-autonomous AI systems identify, evaluate, compare, and potentially act on information, products, or brands on a user's behalf. It extends the question from whether AI mentions a brand to whether an AI agent chooses it.

Why this matters

It moves the commercial question from mention to selection. An agent that shortlists on a buyer's behalf is making a purchase decision, not a visibility one.

RelatedAI RecommendationMachine GatekeeperAI-Mediated Discovery

Answer Eligibility

Whether a source is even in the running to be used in an answer. Industry Term

Answer eligibility describes whether a source contains information that is relevant, accessible, and clear enough to be considered for inclusion in an AI-generated answer. A brand cannot be recognized if it cannot first be retrieved.

Why this matters

It is the floor beneath everything else. Authority work has no effect on a page a system cannot reach, read, or make sense of.

RelatedRetrievabilityRetrievalSearch Engine Optimization (SEO)

AI Overviews

Google's AI-generated summaries above traditional search results. Industry Term

AI Overviews are Google's AI-generated summaries that appear above traditional search results for some queries, drawing on and citing a range of web sources.

Why this matters

It is where most people first met AI-mediated discovery, and it sits on top of the same index as ordinary search, which is why search fundamentals still matter.

RelatedAI SearchAI ModeCitation

AI Mode

Google's conversational search experience, built on query fan-out. Industry Term

AI Mode is Google's conversational AI search experience, which uses query fan-out to research a question across multiple searches before generating a response.

Why this matters

Because it researches across many underlying searches, a brand needs presence across a topic rather than a single well-optimized page.

RelatedQuery Fan-OutConversational SearchAI Overviews

03 · How Understanding Forms

Retrieval, Evidence & Consensus

Information exists, then it is retrieved, then sources are selected, then they corroborate one another, then consensus emerges. This is where Aingworth's Consensus Over Rankings thesis lives.

View all 16 terms

Retrieval

Finding the relevant information a system will use to answer. Technical Term

Retrieval is the process an AI system uses to find relevant information from available sources before generating a response.

Why this matters

It is the step where a brand is either in the running or not. Everything downstream, citation, description, recommendation, only happens to sources that were retrieved.

RelatedRetrievabilityRetrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)Source Selection

Retrievability

The degree to which a brand's information can actually be found and used. Aingworth Concept

Retrievability is the degree to which a brand's information can be found, understood, and surfaced by the systems that retrieve information for search and AI-generated answers. Visibility is an outcome. Retrievability is the underlying condition that makes it possible.

Why this matters

It is the bridge between technical work and the recognition argument. A brand cannot be recognized if its information cannot first be found and understood.

RelatedRetrievalAnswer EligibilitySearch Engine Optimization (SEO)

Query Fan-Out

Breaking one question into many searches before answering. Industry Term

Query fan-out is the process of breaking a complex question into multiple related searches or subtopics so an AI system can gather information from a wider range of sources before constructing an answer.

Why this matters

One buyer question can generate many underlying searches. That rewards brands recognized across a network of related topics and penalizes those optimized for a single keyword.

RelatedAI ModeContent ClustersTopical Authority

Source Selection

How a system decides which sources are worth using. Industry Term

Source selection is how a search or AI system decides which of the available sources are relevant and trustworthy enough to use when constructing an answer.

Why this matters

It reframes the strategic question from how to rank to why a system would choose this source over another, which is a far more useful thing to work on.

RelatedSource DiversityMachine GatekeeperAuthority Signal

Source Diversity

The range of independent sources feeding a system's understanding. Industry Term

Source diversity is the range of independent sources contributing to an AI system's understanding of a brand or topic. A claim on only one site is evidence. The same claim confirmed across several independent sources is corroboration.

Why this matters

It explains why publishing more on your own site has diminishing returns. Ten pages on one domain is still one source.

RelatedCorroborationThird-Party EvidenceComposite Picture

Canonical Source

The version of a fact a system treats as authoritative. Industry Term

A canonical source is the source a brand identifies, or that a system treats, as the authoritative version of a particular fact, claim, or description, used when other representations conflict.

Why this matters

If a company has five different descriptions across five places, something has to decide which one is true. A brand that has not made that decision has left it to the system.

RelatedSignal ConsistencyEntity ConsistencyInformation Provenance

Corroboration

Independent sources confirming the same thing. Aingworth Concept

Corroboration happens when independent sources provide consistent evidence supporting the same understanding of a brand, claim, or fact.

Why this matters

It is the mechanism behind Consensus Over Rankings. Saying something on your own website is a claim. The web confirming it independently is evidence.

RelatedConsensus Over RankingsSource DiversityThird-Party Evidence

Information Provenance

The traceable origin and history of a claim. Industry Term

Information provenance is the traceable origin and history of a piece of information: where a claim came from, and what sources support it.

Why this matters

Claims that can be traced to an origin survive scrutiny. Claims that circulate without a source are the ones that get dropped or distorted.

RelatedCanonical SourceCorroborationEarned Authority

Citation

A reference a system uses, and often shows, when answering. Industry Term

A citation is a reference to a brand, source, or piece of information that an AI system or search engine uses, and often surfaces, when constructing an answer.

Why this matters

Citations are the most visible evidence that a system considered a source credible enough to lean on, which makes them the closest observable proxy for recognition.

RelatedCitation ShareAI ReferralCitation Decay

Citation Decay

References becoming fewer, less prominent, or out of date over time. Industry Term

Citation decay happens when references to a brand or source become less frequent, less prominent, or outdated over time. It is a narrower, measurable version of Authority Decay.

Why this matters

It is the early warning. Citations usually thin out before a brand notices any drop in how often it is named.

RelatedAuthority DecayCitationAuthority Measurement

Consensus

Independent sources and systems arriving at the same understanding. Industry Term

Consensus is the degree to which independent sources, and increasingly independent AI systems, arrive at the same understanding of a brand.

Why this matters

Recognition compounds when sources converge. A brand described the same way everywhere is far harder for any single system to get wrong.

RelatedConsensus Over RankingsModel ConsensusCorroboration

Model Consensus

Different AI systems agreeing on what a brand is and is known for. Aingworth Concept

Model consensus is the degree to which different AI systems, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity, arrive at similar understandings of a brand, its expertise, and its relevance.

Why this matters

Agreement across independent models is a stronger signal than performance in any one of them, and it is far less vulnerable to a single platform changing how it works.

RelatedConsensusSignal ConsistencyAI Visibility Measurement

Signal Consistency

Whether the evidence around a brand tells the same story everywhere. Aingworth Concept

Signal consistency is the degree to which the information and evidence around a brand describe the same identity, expertise, and positioning across different sources and platforms. It is what makes a Composite Picture coherent instead of contradictory.

Why this matters

Inconsistency does not average out. It gives a system permission to choose, and it will not always choose the version a brand would have picked.

RelatedComposite PictureEntity ConsistencyCanonical Source

Entity Consistency

Being represented the same way as an entity across the ecosystem. Industry Term

Entity consistency is the degree to which a brand, organization, or person is represented consistently, in name, description, and detail, across the digital ecosystem.

Why this matters

It is the technical half of signal consistency. Mismatched names, addresses, and descriptions make it harder for systems to confirm that every reference points at the same organization.

RelatedSignal ConsistencyEntity SignalsEntity Recognition

Grounding

Connecting a generated answer to real, checkable information. Technical Term

Grounding is how an AI system connects a generated response to external, verifiable information, rather than relying only on patterns learned during training.

Why this matters

Grounded answers draw on sources that exist now, which is what makes current, well-structured published evidence worth building.

RelatedRetrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)RetrievalAI Hallucination

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

Retrieving source material before generating an answer. Technical Term

Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG, is a technique where an AI system retrieves relevant information from an external source before generating a response, so the answer is grounded in current information rather than training data alone.

Why this matters

It is the reason publishing still works. If systems only used training data, nothing published today could affect an answer for years.

RelatedRetrievalGroundingVector Search

04 · The Disciplines

Search, SEO & Generative Optimization

The established disciplines brands use to improve discoverability. Useful, and often necessary, but tactics that sit on top of a brand's underlying authority infrastructure rather than replace it.

View all 11 terms

Search Engine Optimization (SEO)

Improving a site so search engines can index, understand, and rank it. Industry Term

Search Engine Optimization, or SEO, is the practice of improving a website so that traditional search engines can index, understand, and rank it. It remains the technical foundation that generative and answer engines build on.

Why this matters

AI features sit on top of the same index as ordinary search. A page that cannot be crawled or understood is not eligible for either, which is why the fundamentals have not gone away.

RelatedRetrievabilityAnswer EligibilityGenerative Engine Optimization (GEO)

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)

Optimizing content for representation in generative AI environments. Industry Term

Generative Engine Optimization, or GEO, refers to practices intended to improve how a brand is represented within generative AI and answer-engine environments. GEO can be a useful tactical discipline, but sustainable recognition depends on the authority infrastructure behind the content, not only on optimizing it for an AI interface.

Why this matters

GEO describes a set of tactics, not a position. Treating it as the whole strategy leaves a brand optimizing content for an interface while the authority underneath it stays thin.

RelatedSearch Engine Optimization (SEO)Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)Brand Authority Infrastructure

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)

Structuring content so answer engines can extract and use it. Industry Term

Answer Engine Optimization, or AEO, refers to structuring content so that answer engines can understand, extract, and use it when responding to a question. It is a useful tactical discipline within a broader recognition strategy, not a replacement for one.

Why this matters

Making content extractable is genuinely useful work. It just determines how easily a system can use a source, not whether it trusts it enough to want to.

RelatedAnswer EngineGenerative Engine Optimization (GEO)Structured Data

Semantic Search

Search that interprets meaning rather than matching keywords. Technical Term

Semantic search is a search approach that interprets the meaning and intent behind a query, rather than matching keywords alone.

Why this matters

It is why keyword density stopped working. Systems now match on meaning, which rewards clear, well-defined language over repetition.

RelatedEmbeddingsSemantic OptimizationVector Search

Semantic Optimization

Structuring content so its meaning, not just its keywords, is clear. Industry Term

Semantic optimization is the practice of structuring content so its meaning, not just its keywords, is clear to search and AI systems, including the entities, relationships, and context involved.

Why this matters

It is the practical response to semantic search: be explicit about what you mean, who you are talking about, and how the ideas relate.

RelatedSemantic SearchEntity RecognitionStructured Data

Entity Recognition

Identifying and classifying real-world things within text. Technical Term

Entity recognition is the process by which a system identifies and classifies real-world things, such as a person, brand, or place, within text or data.

Why this matters

Before a system can attach any authority to a brand, it has to reliably pick that brand out of text and tell it apart from similarly named ones.

RelatedEntityEntity ConsistencyKnowledge Graph

Entity Authority

The strength and consistency of the signals tied to an entity. Industry Term

Entity authority describes the strength and consistency of the signals connecting an entity to its identity, expertise, relationships, and reputation across the digital ecosystem. Strong entity signals help systems distinguish who a brand is and what it is known for.

Why this matters

It is the technical vocabulary closest to Earned Authority, and a useful bridge when talking to search teams who think in entities rather than in brand terms.

RelatedEarned AuthorityEntity SignalsKnowledge Graph

Knowledge Graph

A structured map of entities and how they relate. Technical Term

A knowledge graph is a structured database of entities and the relationships between them, used by search and AI systems to understand how people, brands, and concepts connect.

Why this matters

Presence in a knowledge graph is one of the strongest forms of structural authority, because it is where a system stores what it considers settled about an entity.

RelatedStructural AuthorityEntityEntity Recognition

Structured Data

Information formatted so machines can read it directly. Technical Term

Structured data is information organized in a standardized format so machines can read and interpret it directly, rather than inferring it from unstructured text.

Why this matters

Common Questions

Questions people ask about this vocabulary.

What is the difference between AI visibility and recognition?
AI visibility measures how often a brand appears in AI-generated answers. Recognition asks whether the brand is understood correctly enough to be trusted and chosen. A brand can be visible and still be misrepresented. Aingworth treats visibility as one signal of recognition, not the goal itself.
Is GEO the same as SEO?
No. SEO is the practice of optimizing a website for traditional search engines. GEO refers to practices aimed specifically at generative AI and answer engines. Aingworth treats both as tactical disciplines that sit on top of a brand's underlying authority infrastructure, not as the strategy itself.
What is the Recognition Triangle?
The Recognition Triangle is a framework by Lucy Aingworth defining the three conditions AI systems use to recognize and cite a brand: Clarity (what a brand stands for, stated without ambiguity), Credibility (what can be verified), and Earned Authority (what others say when the brand is not in the room). Recognition forms only where all three hold.
What is Consensus Over Rankings?
Consensus Over Rankings is Aingworth's central thesis: that being confirmed by independent, converging sources matters more than ranking first on any single one. AI systems form an understanding from many sources rather than returning one ranked list, so a brand's job is to be the consistent answer across all of them.
Where do the Aingworth terms on this page come from?
Most of the framework-level terms, the Recognition Triangle, the Authority Method, the Machine Gatekeeper, Authority Debt, the Recognition Economy, and the Composite Picture, were introduced by Lucy Aingworth in Become the Answer: How Brands Build Authority in the Age of AI (2026). Established industry and technical terms are defined here in plain language, and marked as industry or technical terms rather than Aingworth concepts.
