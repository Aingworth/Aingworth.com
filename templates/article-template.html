What generative engine optimisation actually is

GEO is not SEO with a new label. It is the practice of becoming the source an AI system reaches for when it answers on your behalf.

Lucy Aingworth
AI Visibility Consultant
Published 13 Jul 2026 Updated 20 Jul 2026 5 min read

The short answer

Generative engine optimisation (GEO) is the practice of structuring a brand's content and signals so AI answer systems such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews can recognise, verify, and cite it. Where SEO optimised for ranking in a list of links, GEO optimises for being retrieved and quoted as a source inside the answer itself.

Key takeaways

  • AI answer engines choose sources before the user sees anything, so the goal shifts from being findable to being retrievable.
  • Citability is structural: clear entity definitions, verifiable claims, self-contained passages, and structured data.
  • GEO builds on SEO rather than replacing it, since most AI citations still go to pages that already rank organically.

Recognition Triangle

The Recognition Triangle is the framework introduced by Lucy Aingworth in Become the Answer, defining the three conditions AI systems use to decide who to cite: Clarity, Credibility, and Earned Authority. Recognition forms only where all three hold.

Common questions

Is GEO just SEO with a new name?
No. SEO optimises content to rank as a link in a results list that a human scans. GEO optimises content to be retrieved, verified, and cited inside an AI-generated answer. They overlap heavily, and strong SEO remains the foundation, but the objective and the formats differ.
What does GEO stand for?
GEO stands for generative engine optimisation: optimising for generative AI systems such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Google AI Overviews.
