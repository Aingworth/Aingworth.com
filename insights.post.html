What Generative Engine Optimisation Actually Is

GEO is not SEO with a new label. It is the practice of becoming the source an AI system reaches for when it answers on your behalf.

By Lucy Aingworth · Published 13 July 2026 · Updated 14 July 2026 · 5 min read

The short answer

Generative engine optimisation (GEO) is the practice of structuring a brand's content and signals so AI answer systems such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews can recognise, verify, and cite it in their answers. Where SEO optimised for ranking in a list of links, GEO optimises for being retrieved and quoted as a source.

Key takeaways

  • AI answer engines choose sources before the user sees anything, so the goal shifts from being findable to being retrievable.
  • Citability is structural: clear entity definitions, verifiable claims, self-contained passages, and structured data.
  • GEO builds on SEO rather than replacing it; the large majority of AI citations still come from pages that already rank organically.
  • Shortcuts do not transfer. Answer engines are built to surface genuine, verifiable authority.

Generative engine optimisation, or GEO, is the practice of making a brand recognisable and citable to the AI systems that now answer questions on people's behalf. When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI which option to choose, GEO is the work that decides whether your brand is in the answer.

It gets confused with SEO constantly. They overlap, but they are not the same job.

What changed between search and answers?

The change is who does the choosing. Traditional search returns a list and the person picks. An answer engine picks first: it synthesises a response and cites a handful of sources, often without the person ever seeing a list.

There is no page two. There is frequently no page one in the old sense at all. Either the system reaches for you when it composes the answer, or it does not. That shifts the objective from being findable to being retrievable: the source a system trusts enough to build its answer on.

How is GEO different from SEO?

GEO builds on SEO rather than replacing it. Strong organic performance remains the entry ticket, since the overwhelming majority of AI citations go to pages that already rank. The difference is what each practice optimises for once the fundamentals hold.

SEO GEO
Optimises for Ranking as a link Being cited in an answer
Judged by Ranking algorithms Language models retrieving sources
Unit of success The click The citation
Content format Pages built for scanning Self-contained, quotable passages
Key signals Keywords, backlinks Entity clarity, verifiability, structure

What does GEO actually involve?

GEO is less about keywords and more about being legible to a machine as a distinct, credible entity. In practice the work falls into four areas:

  • Clarity of entity. The system has to be able to say what you are without ambiguity, consistently, everywhere it encounters you.
  • Structured, verifiable signals. Schema, clean data, and claims that can be confirmed rather than merely asserted.
  • Earned citation. Being referenced by other credible sources, because answer engines lean heavily on what the wider web already treats as authoritative.
  • Content built to be quoted. Clear, self-contained explanations a system can lift a passage from and attribute to you.

What is GEO not?

GEO is not a trick, and it is not a new set of tags you bolt on to fool a model. Answer engines are built to surface genuine authority, which means the shortcuts that once worked in SEO actively hurt you here. There is no keyword density that makes a system trust you.

It is also not separate from your actual reputation. GEO is what happens when real authority is made legible to a machine. You cannot optimise your way into being cited as the answer if you are not, in fact, a credible answer. The work is building the authority, then making sure the systems can see it.

Questions people ask

Is GEO the same as SEO?
No. SEO optimises content to rank as a link in a results list that a human scans. GEO optimises content to be retrieved, verified, and cited inside an AI-generated answer. They overlap heavily, and strong SEO remains the foundation, but the objective and the formats differ.
What does GEO stand for?
GEO stands for generative engine optimisation: optimising for generative AI systems such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Google AI Overviews. You will also see the terms AEO (answer engine optimisation) and AI search optimisation used for broadly the same practice.
Does my brand need GEO if it already ranks well on Google?
Yes. Ranking makes you a candidate, but AI systems cite only a fraction of the pages they retrieve. Structure, verifiability, and entity clarity decide whether a well-ranked page actually gets quoted in the answer.
How do I check my brand's AI visibility?
Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI the questions your buyers ask, in a logged-out session, and note whether you are cited, described accurately, or absent. Repeat across several phrasings and platforms, and look for patterns rather than reading too much into any single answer.
